The two tabs along the very top: `tasks` at the left edge and `agenda` at the right edge, each as wide as half the screen and `touch` tall. The one you are on is set in `ink`; the other is `quiet`, a little faded. The words are not set in a font: like the counts they are drawn on slanted bars, fourteen to a letter, with the unlit bars faintly showing, so they read as capitals like on a calculator watch. There is no underline, no pill and no divider: the colour is the whole signal.

Each word's text starts at `gutter` from its screen edge. A touch switches tabs. So does sweeping a finger sideways across empty space: to the left on tasks, to the right on the agenda. It only goes one way from each tab, so it never loops, and a sweep that starts on a row is left alone because on a row it means rename or remove. Holding the tasks tab opens its small menu (export and import) just under it; the menu is as wide as its two words, with `space-3` around each, on `panel` with a `stroke-thin` `line` border, and it closes on a touch outside or on back.

Under the tab bar sits the screen's own bar: the counts at the left and the controls at the right (arrow, mode dot and bin on tasks; the bin on the agenda).

The consumer provides which tab is on and what switching does.

- Do keep the order fixed: tasks always left, agenda always right.
- Don't add icons or badges to the tabs; the counts live in the bar below.
